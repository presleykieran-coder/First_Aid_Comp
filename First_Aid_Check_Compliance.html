Why this matters

Your legal duty under UK law

Under the Health and Safety (First Aid) Regulations 1981, every UK employer must make adequate first aid provision. 'Adequate' depends on your workforce, sector, and specific risks — the HSE can investigate at any time following an incident or complaint.

⚠️
Non-compliance can result in: HSE Improvement or Prohibition Notices, prosecution under the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974, unlimited fines, and personal liability for directors and managers.
Section 1

Know your legal baseline

Every employer must carry out a First Aid Needs Assessment — the starting point for all workplace first aid arrangements.

Your assessment must consider:

  • The nature of the work and hazards involved
  • Workforce size and whether you operate across multiple sites
  • Working patterns — shifts, lone workers, remote staff
  • Members of the public on site and any employees with known medical conditions
Legal sources Health and Safety (First Aid) Regulations 1981  ·  HSE ACoP L74  ·  Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 — Section 2

Section 4

Mental health first aid

The HSE expects employers to address mental health under their duty of care. Over 50% of work-related ill health cases involve stress, anxiety, or depression.

1 in 4 UK adults experience a mental health problem each year
50%+ of work-related ill health is stress, anxiety & depression

Section 6

If you've found gaps

  • 1
    Expired certificates Arrange requalification training as a priority — one of the most common issues found during inspections.
  • 2
    Insufficient first aider numbers Review your headcount and risk level, then book appropriate training.
  • 3
    No Mental Health First Aider Enrol a suitable staff member on an accredited MHFA course.
  • 4
    No documented Needs Assessment This is the legal foundation — without it, everything else is unverifiable.
  • 5
    Missing records or accident book Implement a simple recording system. Gaps in documentation may be treated as gaps in compliance.
