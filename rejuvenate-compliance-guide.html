The legal reality

Your duty under UK law

Under the Health and Safety (First Aid) Regulations 1981, every UK employer must make adequate first aid provision. 'Adequate' is defined by your workforce, sector, and specific risks — and the HSE can investigate at any time following an incident or complaint.

⚠️
Non-compliance can result in: HSE Improvement or Prohibition Notices, prosecution under the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974, unlimited fines, and personal liability for directors and managers.
Section 1

Know your legal baseline

Every employer must complete a First Aid Needs Assessment — the legal foundation for everything else.

Your assessment must consider:

  • The nature of the work and hazards involved
  • Workforce size and whether you operate across multiple sites
  • Working patterns — shifts, lone workers, remote staff
  • Members of the public on site and employees with known medical conditions
Legal sources Health and Safety (First Aid) Regulations 1981 · HSE ACoP L74 · Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 — Section 2

Beyond the legal minimum

Mental health at work isn't optional anymore

Mental Health First Aid is not currently a standalone legal requirement — but the HSE strongly recommends it as part of meeting your broader duty of care under the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974. Stress, anxiety, and burnout are among the most common causes of long-term absence, and if you can't demonstrate you've taken reasonable steps to address mental wellbeing, you're exposed.

More importantly: it works. Having a trained Mental Health First Aider on your team means someone is equipped to spot the early signs, have the right conversations, and signpost colleagues to support — before a crisis point.

  • Reduces long-term sickness absence and presenteeism
  • Demonstrates duty of care to staff and to insurers
  • Builds a culture where people feel safe to speak up early
  • Shows the HSE you're taking mental health seriously — not just ticking a box
  • Strengthens your employer brand and staff retention

Section 6

If you've found gaps

  • 1
    Expired certificates Arrange requalification training as a priority — expired certs are one of the most common issues found during HSE inspections.
  • 2
    Insufficient first aider numbers Review your headcount and risk level, then book appropriate physical first aid training.
  • 3
    No Mental Health First Aider Enrol a suitable member of staff on an accredited MHFA course. It's the single highest-impact step most businesses are missing.
  • 4
    No documented Needs Assessment This is the legal foundation — without it, everything else is unverifiable.
  • 5
    Missing records or accident book Gaps in documentation are treated as gaps in compliance by the HSE.
